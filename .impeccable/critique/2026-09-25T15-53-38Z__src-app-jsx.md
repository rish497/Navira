## Specificity verdict

NAVIRA's route geometry, evidence ledgers, hard rules, graphite/bone/vermilion palette, and protected-path mark are product-specific. The interchangeable pattern is architectural: eleven modules repeat a large editorial statement plus one broad panel, while several consequential controls only change a label or show a toast. The deterministic detector reported zero findings in `src/App.jsx`; browser and source evidence identified semantic, contrast, target-size, and workflow gaps outside that scan.

## Priority issues

1. **P1: durable operational state.** Publishing, assigning, acknowledging, retrying, sharing, and starting guidance need actor, recipient, scope, time, expiry, delivery, and recovery.
2. **P1: response-loop information architecture.** Group modules into Observe, Act, and Assure with persistent desktop labels. Treat civilian guidance as a versioned output channel.
3. **P1: mobile decision integrity.** Preserve severity, freshness, connectivity, team state, ETA, coverage, and overall state; place the action queue before the map.
4. **P1: semantic accessibility.** Add selected and expanded ARIA states, 44px targets, drawer focus management, chart data equivalents, language metadata, and accessible contrast.
5. **P2: task-specific instruments.** Replace repeated broad panels with causal infrastructure, owned/committed resources, threshold-driven analytics, and conflict-aware dispatch.

## Persona red flags

- Incident commanders cannot immediately identify the next operational decision.
- Field dispatchers lose status and ETA on mobile.
- Keyboard and assistive-technology users cannot perceive several active states or safely operate the mobile drawer.
- Civilians receive a clear first instruction but no offline assurance, rerouting, progress, or arrival confirmation.

## Questions

- What is the single action an incident commander must take in the next 30 seconds?
- What durable evidence proves that a route or assignment was received?
- Should civilian guidance be a peer module or a versioned output of command decisions?
